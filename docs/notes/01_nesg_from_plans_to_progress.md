# Reading 1: NESG, From Plans to Progress (Dec 2025) [S02]

## Summary

1. NESG finds real momentum: conversion centres grew from 7 to 250+ and stations from about 11 to 53+ "daughter" stations by mid-2025, with 50,000+ vehicles converted.
2. Uptake is held back by thin refuelling infrastructure (queues, est. 1.5 hrs average and rising), high upfront cost, and a north–south divide in access.
3. Supply is fragile: a 24-hour loading time at the Ajaokuta gas plant disrupts deliveries to stations.
4. Safety incidents come from poorly done conversions and substandard equipment, made worse by weak certification of technicians.
5. NESG calls for regional station targets, vouchers or rebates for conversion, CBN-backed financing, a stable CNG–petrol price gap, independent safety audits, and published, verified, disaggregated data.

## Relevance to this study

- **Data transparency is itself a finding.** NESG notes that numbers can't be verified through official channels and reports conflicting figures (e.g., 8,000 vs 500+ technicians trained).
- **The queue estimate of 1.5 hours** is the low-case input in this study. Reports from 2026 (S04) point much higher.
- **The "stable price differential" recommendation** links directly to the break-even analysis. The Sept 2025 jump from ₦230 to ₦380/scm (S12) shows the differential is not stable.
