# Driver Survey: Method

Source ID: **S47** (see `sources.md`). Cleaning script: `src/clean_survey.py`. Clean data: `data/processed/survey_clean.csv`.

## Purpose

The survey collects first-hand figures from Lagos commercial drivers to check the cost model's assumptions against what drivers report. It covers daily earnings, fuel spend before and after conversion, queue times at CNG stations, and the reasons petrol drivers give for not converting. Published sources give little of this at driver level.

## Instrument

- The questionnaire is a Google Form. The form was generated by a script so that its question wording and order are fixed and reproducible.
- The first question asks for consent to take part. All 25 responses recorded agreement.
- Respondents who answered 'No' to the consent question were taken directly to the end of the form; this routing is set in the form-building script.
- After the shared questions on vehicle, ownership, working days, hours and gross daily earnings, the form branches by current fuel type:
  - **Petrol only:** daily petrol spend, barriers to conversion (tick all that apply), and whether the driver would convert if a station were within 10 minutes or if conversion were free.
  - **CNG only, or CNG and petrol (bi-fuel):** conversion cost and how it was paid, daily CNG spend, daily petrol spend before conversion, refuels per day, queue time per refuel (in bands), how often CNG was unavailable in the last month, and whether conversion has saved money.
- No names, phone numbers or other direct identifiers were collected.

## Fielding

- Responses were collected from 4 to 7 October 2026.
- Recruitment: drivers were approached in person by the researcher at CNG stations and car parks in Lagos. Drivers able to complete the form on their own phones did so (10 responses). For drivers who could not, the researcher asked the questions in person, noted the answers, and entered them into the form on the evening of 7 October 2026 (15 responses).
- 10 responses were self-completed by drivers.
- 15 were interviewer-administered: the researcher asked the questions in person and later entered the answers. They were entered between 21:24 and 21:54 on 7 October 2026; the cleaning script uses this window to set administration_mode.
- Total: 25 responses (12 petrol only, 13 CNG or bi-fuel).

## Cleaning

- The raw export is kept locally and is not published. The published clean file leaves out submission timestamps.
- Queue bands are converted to hours at the band midpoints. The open-ended "More than 6 hours" band is set to 7 hours as an assumption; no respondent chose it.
- Five responses (R09, R15, R17, R22, R25) are flagged for implausible money values. A response is flagged if fuel spend is greater than or equal to gross daily earnings, or if gross daily earnings are below ₦20,000. Their original values are kept in the file. They are excluded from all cost figures reported from the survey.

## Limitations

- **Small, non-random sample.** Twenty-five drivers do not represent Lagos commercial drivers as a whole. Results are indicative only and should not be used for population estimates. Administration mode also differs between respondents, and interviewer-administered answers may differ systematically from self-completed ones.
- **Recalled pre-conversion petrol spend.** CNG users' "before" petrol spend is recalled, possibly from months earlier, and petrol prices changed over that period. This affects the fuel-saving estimate.
- **Bi-fuel drivers' current petrol spend was not asked.** Nine of the 13 CNG users run on both fuels. Their current CNG spend leaves out any petrol they still buy, so the fuel saving for this group is likely overstated.
- **Mostly free-programme converters.** Most CNG users converted through the free government programme, so conversion cost in this sample is close to zero. The sample cannot be used to estimate what drivers pay at market conversion prices, and the savings they report do not include repaying a conversion loan.
- Interviewer-recorded answers were transcribed after collection, so transcription errors are possible. Recruitment at CNG stations and car parks means the sample reflects drivers found at those locations, not a random sample.
